**The process** 

Following a short telephone conversation to review the project requirements and site specifics, we will schedule data collection. Field data collection can often happen within a few days of contract agreement signing. 

Data Collection

One or two “reality capture specialists” will arrive at the house in the morning and begin external scanning and photography. Scanning involves 80 or so individual “shots” using a tripod mounted LiDAR (Light Detection and Ranging), which spins in two directions taking millions of measurements. This unit is completely safe and quiet.

A second set of data is collected using a 360° camera that captures a spherical photo allowing the viewer to spin the resulting image and look in any direction. 200+ photos are taken to record the interior and exterior of the property. 

Data collection takes between 3-5 hours about 2 hours of which are inside the house.  The house does not have to be neat and owners or tenants are often in the house so we make every effort to stay out of the way and respect privacy and daily routines.

Data Cleaning and Assembly  
Following data collection the next day is spent assembling the individual scans into a single “registered” point cloud. A point cloud is a dimensionally accurate, detailed 3D model of the house expressed in colored dots. The point cloud can be used by sophisticated Architects, Designers and Contractors directly but most project require that we convert the point cloud to a CAD (2D) or BIM (3D) model, and a 2D PDF set that can be printed at a correct scale. 

In parallel, the 360° photos are sorted by location on the site, floor and room for easy access. These photos will be stored on a shared drive so the client can download them and share them with architects, designers, contractors and friends.

In some cases we will fly a drone above the house to provide roof details and locations of vents and skylights.

Drawing and Model Generation

The next (and longest) step in the process involves translating the point cloud into plans, elevations, sections and, when required, 3D models.

Finally, the plans and models are reviewed, assembled into a printable PDF set and stored on a shared drive for download and sharing. 

For a 3,000 square foot house including attic and basic site documentation, the process typically takes 5-6 days to complete following data collection.

2.7 	Content:   
**Deliverables**

Ground Truth generated Existing Conditions Documents with a wide range of detailing depending on the client needs. The majority are used as an accurate base model for architects, designers and contractors who build from the model, make changes and add details to create design drawings, bid drawings and construction documents. 

Every project includes the following basic assets:

* 2D Site Plan \- Including the building(s), driveway, hardscape and retaining walls immediately adjacent to the building(s)  
* 2D Floor Plans  
* 2D Reflected Ceiling Plans (RCP) including locations of changes in ceiling heights, soffits, beams and other changes in the ceiling plane and light fixture locations  
* 2D Roof Plans \- Including skylights, chimney(s), and visible vents  
* 2D Exterior Elevations indicating sill and head height for doors and windows but excluding window division details and casing  
* 2D Sections \- Including 1 “long” section typical selected to show significant changes in ceiling and floor levels or important details, and 1 “cross” section  
* If included in the project scope, garages, pool houses, sheds, trellises and other garden features can be included.


	When 3D models are required, additional modeling is required to generate a complete 3D model of the contracted structures. 3D models are usually generated without materials, and trim detailing but include schematic representations of kitchen and bath cabinets and fixtures.

These “base” 3D models are ideal as input to architectural and interior design planning and work well with new AI design rendering programs.
