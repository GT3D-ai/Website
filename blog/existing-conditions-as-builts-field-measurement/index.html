Existing conditions, as-builts, and field measurement

Clear language for three ideas that often get used interchangeably

September 22, 2025

In conversations with owners and designers, “as-built” can mean a redlined PDF, a survey, a Revit model, or simply “someone went to the site and wrote things down.” That ambiguity is where schedules slip and RFIs multiply—because nobody aligned on what “done” looks like.

Field measurement is the act of capturing dimensions and relationships—tapes, total stations, laser scans, or photogrammetry. It produces evidence, not necessarily a formatted drawing set.

Existing conditions documentation is the broader product: organized deliverables (2D, 3D, point clouds, photos) that describe what is built today, structured so design and construction teams can work from them.

As-builts in the traditional sense often means record drawings updated to reflect construction completion. In renovation work, teams frequently need existing conditions before design starts—which is a different scope from post-construction as-builts even if the same tools are involved.

Practical takeaway

Start by naming the decision the documentation needs to support—permit, demo, MEP routing, lease area, or owner reserve study—and let that drive format and LOD. We’re happy to translate “as-built” into a checklist everyone can sign.
