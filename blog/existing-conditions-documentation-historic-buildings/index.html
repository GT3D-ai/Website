Existing Conditions Documentation for Historic Buildings

Why high-fidelity as-builts matter when every opening and detail carries preservation weight

March 1, 2026

Historic buildings are rarely “as drawn.” Decades of alterations, undocumented repairs, and material changes mean that relying on old plans alone is a risk for structural engineers, preservation architects, and code consultants alike.

Existing conditions documentation grounded in LiDAR and 360° photography establishes a single, verifiable baseline: wall thicknesses, floor-to-floor heights, window and door openings, and the relationships between structural and ornamental elements. That baseline becomes the common reference for schematic design, life-safety upgrades, accessibility improvements, and grant-backed preservation work.

What teams do with the deliverables

  • Compare proposed interventions against measured geometry before construction starts.
  • Share a consistent digital record with reviewers, contractors, and stakeholders who may never visit the site together.
  • Reduce change orders driven by surprises in the field.

When the goal is to protect character while meeting modern performance requirements, the first step is knowing—precisely—what exists today. That’s the work we’re built to support.
